Straight-line CVT vehicle acceleration model
============================================

Purpose
-------
Estimate acceleration performance from vehicle, engine, CVT, driveline, tire,
and event-load inputs. Vehicle and wheel speeds evolve independently. The
engine revs from idle to 2,400 RPM, then drives an ideal slipping CVT
engagement until its belt speed matches the driven side.

States and integration
----------------------
The runner advances vehicle speed, wheel speed, and longitudinal tire force with
explicit Euler steps. Engine speed is a dynamic state while revving freely,
held at 2,400 RPM during launch slip, and constrained by wheel speed and CVT
ratio after belt lock. Steps are split at engagement and belt lock. Tire force
uses an exact first-order relaxation update toward its Burckhardt steady-state
target. Distance is integrated with the trapezoid rule.

At each step
------------
1. Compute aerodynamic drag, rolling resistance, grade force, and event loads.
2. Compute the distance-ramped brake load, if configured.
3. Hold maximum CVT reduction until the belt locks. After lock, select a
   bounded CVT ratio to target peak-power RPM.
4. Compute engine torque and CVT engagement torque, then CVT efficiency and
   downstream stage torques in sequence. In the locked mode, engine inertia
   and the ratio-change term enter the coupled engine/wheel acceleration solve.
5. Treat all four driven tires as one equivalent wheel with shared speed and
total driveline torque. Each tire carries one quarter of vehicle weight; sum
all driven tire loads and inertias. Burckhardt B/C/D parameters define the
steady friction curve.
Actual force approaches that target over the configured relaxation length.
6. Advance vehicle, wheel, engine, and tire-force states. Vehicle speed is
   clamped at zero; distance uses the trapezoid rule.

Scope and limitations
---------------------
- CVT ratio selection is instantaneous and follows the launch/peak-power rule.
- The engine starts at 1,800 RPM idle and revs freely to 2,400 RPM. While the
  engagement slips at 2,400 RPM, all available engine torque passes through the
  CVT at maximum reduction. Lock occurs when engine and driven belt speeds
  match, without an instantaneous speed jump.
- Belt-slip and tire-slip power losses are implicit in the dynamics; their heat
  is not tracked as a state.
- The tire model is a simplified longitudinal, load-independent empirical curve;
  its terrain parameters and 0.2 m relaxation length are representative inputs,
  not measured ATV-specific data.
- At very low speed the relaxation speed is floored at 0.5 m/s so tire force can
  build during launch.
- No load transfer, belt torque capacity, belt thermal behavior, or finite CVT
  shift rate is included. The torque transmitted at engagement starts abruptly.
- The pilot-tow event applies 100 N of braking force per foot traveled and
  stops its run after the vehicle remains stationary for one second.
